## Overview

**AI15 turns an approved and evaluated model into an actually operating, independently observable and recoverable AI service.** The learner must package immutable model and data-processing artifacts, operate a real locally reachable inference interface, quantify latency and capacity under load, instrument privacy-safe service observability, set meaningful SLOs/error budgets, execute safe candidate/shadow/canary promotion, detect live training–serving skew and composed RAG/agent consistency errors, and recover from denied, overloaded, timed-out or partially completed operations. **Running a notebook once, printing a 'deployed' message, publishing weights or obtaining a high model benchmark score does not meet the service operations outcomes.** Operational quality is an *observed service-and-user fact*, bounded by the tested population and runtime.

**Entry readiness and course ownership.** The **direct prerequisite is completed [AI14](AI14.md)**, which inherits [AI13](AI13.md) cross-system evaluation, [AI10](AI10.md) adaptation and model artifacts, [AI11](AI11.md) evidence-grounded RAG, [AI12](AI12.md) tool-dispatch/verified state, AI9 generative inference, earlier data/statistical/SEF preparation, and AI14's authorized risk and governance decisions. **AI15 does not repeat** generic neural optimization, RAG relevance mathematics, agent planning, beginner software testing or AI14 threat/fairness/rights policy. Its genuinely new capability is *system operation*: packaged model serving, live request and queue/resource contracts, reliability engineering, instrumentation, registry/version promotion, incident/safe rollback, training–serving skew and sustainable lifecycle economics. APP and SYS have deeper general application/networking/distributed-operation specialties; **no APP/SYS/SEC course is a hidden prerequisite** for the compulsory CPU-local HTTP and simulated capacity work. If a learner elects to deploy at cloud or high GPU scale, additional specialized operations skills and platform cost/permissions may be needed, but that is not an OEE AI15 completion requirement.

**Boundary to AI14 and AI16.** AI14 owns whether an AI system *should* be operated, what rights and harms matter and who may authorize residual risk. AI15 must **enforce those approved limits** in a real locally operating service, provide actionable live evidence and demonstrate verified recovery or safe hold; it cannot substitute new legal certification or claim universal model safety. AI13 owns independent test and statistical validity, which AI15 reuses at release/rollback gates without rebuilding its whole evaluation course. AI16 integrates the full AI program in a separate capstone, not another AI15 service module. AI17–AI20 electives remain optional and cannot become hidden AI15 prerequisites.

**Affordable, honest compulsory environment.** Every required substantial work item is reproducible from a clean checkout on an ordinary CPU using a small open/synthetic licensed model, simple HTTP or equivalent independently callable transport, a local client/load harness, a file or in-memory version registry, a synthetic RAG source or actor-aware tool where appropriate, and transparent timestamps/counters. A toy model must actually compute a result and show observable input dependence; an entirely canned "OK" endpoint cannot substitute for measured inference. The student may implement using a mainstream open Python/TypeScript/Rust stack with appropriate accessible source and dependency instructions; no fixed language or orchestrator is mandated. No paid endpoint, foundation-model license, GPU, private organization data, AWS/Kubernetes/Prometheus/MLflow account, shared server, live dangerous external action or professional on-call team is compulsory. Multi-node scheduling, accelerator-specific serving or external provider fees may be analyzed using explicit simulations and current optional documentation, never invented as results actually observed. All charts and tracing visualizations must have readable numerical/text alternatives.

**Academic derivation and workload.** The sequence follows actual operating dependencies: independently validated artifacts/API before a real service; measurable request and queue behavior before SLIs/SLOs; privacy-safe instrumentation before incident detection; approved immutable releases before canary and verified rollback; versioned feature/RAG/agent integrity before trustworthy online feedback; bounded recovery before lifecycle economics; and a single reproducible unfamiliar on-call defense. A mechanism and its immediate adverse/verification case share a lesson when they establish the same skill, while model serving, queue math, telemetry, release authority, versioned composed-system state and incident recovery retain separate proofs. **No number of modules, lessons or projects is a design target.** Eight proportional cumulative assessments evaluate twenty-seven observable outcomes and may reuse a single genuine service/evidence collection; there is no extra mandatory project per module. A provisional **18–24 weeks** includes implementing model endpoints, independent numerical derivations, load experiment design, debugging, instrumentation, stress/rollback checks, analysis and technical defense. Learner-tested workload or promotion to a higher OEE status has **not** been established.

## Completion requirements

### Public independently checkable service and mathematical reference cases

These cases specify **hypothetical valid assumptions**, precise denominators and separately observable effects. None is a claim about real service speed, cloud pricing, GPU throughput, reliability of a trained model, security certification or deployed population behavior. All service experiments must retain actual input/rights, version, tool/feature/index identity, clock timing and clean-state source for independent review.

**V1 — Artifact, schema and serving readiness.** Suppose an authorized small classifier artifact has immutable **model v1 digest `m7`**, feature transform v2 and request schema v1; a candidate claims model v1 but resolves digest `m9` or a changed feature transform. Under an explicitly trusted manifest, it must **not be marked ready or promoted**. A service receiving schema v1 with `{"x":12}` may return a *genuinely computed* model-dependent numeric prediction and exact revision; `{"x":"12"}` is invalid under a strict-number contract, and a request exceeding the documented input size is rejected before computation. An HTTP process may be *live* while its model is still loading or its authorized source is unavailable, so live does **not** imply ready. Digests `m7` and `m9` are schematic IDs in this teaching case, not real cryptographic hashes.

**P1 — Tail latency uses an explicit percentile convention.** Ten observed/request-level end-to-end latency values in milliseconds are already sorted: **[40,50,60,70,80,90,110,200,400,900]**. Under *nearest-rank* percentile indexing `ceil(p×n)` with 1-based ranks, **p50=80 ms**, **p95=900 ms** and **p99=900 ms**. A linearly interpolated library percentile would produce different values and is not interchangeable without declaring its method. Ten points do not estimate a production population's true 95th- or 99th-percentile latency with confidence. For streaming generation, time to first token and end-to-end completion/decoded output rate need separate timestamps and units; a fabricated initial byte is not a generated token.

**Q1 — Ideal capacity versus stable observed queue.** A **hypothetical stable system** receives average λ=**12 requests/s**, with observed mean time in the system W=**0.25 seconds** and bounded steady state. Little's Law yields average in-flight population L=λW=**3 requests**. Separately, one ideal serial worker capable of processing at most **8 requests/s** cannot serve a sustained 12/s stream indefinitely without queue growth/rejection; two ideal workers, each independently sustaining8/s with **no shared overhead**, have notional capacity **16/s**. Actual batching, transfer, memory contention and scheduling may lower that rate or increase latency, and must be measured rather than inferred from this arithmetic. The L=3 claim must **not** be applied to the unstable, unlimited-queue one-worker example.

**E1 — Eligible-request SLO and error budget.** In a *stipulated fixed measurement window*, service objective is **99.5% successful eligible requests**, and exactly **10,000 eligible** user requests occur. Allowed failure budget is **(1−.995)×10,000=50** eligible failures. If **80 fail**, successful requests=**9,920**, realized request-success fraction **99.2%**, budget consumption=**80/50=1.6 = 160% of budget**. A budget consumption factor is not itself a rate per hour and should not be confused with a multi-window time-based burn-rate policy without the relevant windows/traffic assumptions. Requests refused by a documented authorization rule cannot be dropped silently from the chosen denominator; whether they are eligible user service errors must be specified up front. A high HTTP success fraction does not imply the AI output was grounded or harmless.

**C1 — Canary control and candidate supports.** Under a stipulated local routed evaluation of exactly **1,000 eligible requests**, control version handles **900** requests, **9 errors**, rate **1%**. Candidate version handles **100**, **8 errors**, rate **8%**. Combined errors=**17**, overall observed error rate **17/1,000=1.7%**. This is conditional on the observed split and traffic; routing labels, time, tenant/task composition and confidence bounds must be checked before a causal or rollout decision. A **shadow** evaluation may produce extra model invocations but its responses/side effects are not user-facing requests and cannot be silently counted as the same denominator. A rollback may change future routing while in-flight requests and already committed tool effects remain on their original versions.

**F1 — Training–serving transformation skew.** The approved training feature contract defines numerical standardized value `z=(x−mean)/scale` with **mean10** and positive **scale2**. For online input **x=12**, correct transformed feature **z=1**. A serving process silently using incompatible transform **mean8, scale2** instead yields **z=2**. Both values look plausible to a model, but the second violates the release's versioned feature semantics; whether the prediction changes must be *actually observed on the selected model*. A missing value and numeric zero are distinct unless explicitly defined, and an unseen category must follow declared schema/error policy.

**I1 — Retry, actor and authorization semantics.** A simulated service calls an approved state-changing tool with `(actor=U,target=R,operation=commit,expected_revision=v2,idempotency_key=k7)`; the tool commits exactly once but the first response is lost. Two transport deliveries with the **identical** authorized operation/key must cause **no more than one** committed mutation under the stated idempotent tool implementation, confirmed by reading authoritative state. Reusing `k7` for a different actor, target, operation or payload is a **collision/invalid request**, not permission to issue a second effect. A client timeout is neither proof of a failed commit nor permission for unlimited retries. Denied tenants cannot read a private source or corresponding cached response, regardless of model confidence or error-body detail.

**K1 — Transparent fictional token-price arithmetic.** Consider **1,000** requests with an average **120 input** and **30 output** tokens, giving **120,000 input** and **30,000 output** tokens. With *illustrative nonlive prices* of **$0.20 per million input tokens** and **$0.80 per million output tokens**, the token-only components are **$0.024** input and **$0.024** output, total **$0.048**. This is **not** a real vendor quote or the cost of CPU/GPU operation: exclude no hidden overhead without disclosing it. The final model/system decision may differ when prompt caching, batch, cloud capacity, storage, network, rate limits and user-visible correctness are accounted for.

**G1 — Hard-stop and rollback verification.** A hypothetical release gate requires (i) exact artifact/source identity and permitted rights, (ii) completed AI13 quality review for the intended population, (iii) AI14 approval/authorization/zero unauthorized effects, (iv) SLO and tail-latency limits and (v) working versioned fallback/rollback. Even if candidate response quality exceeds baseline, a **failed ACL or incompatible tokenizer** means **HOLD**, not canary promotion. During a failing canary, routing *new* requests back to control is only one recovery step: verify real model/source identity and current postconditions, preserve in-flight attribution and never claim reversal of already executed external tool actions. A held or refused deployment can demonstrate full operating competence when release would be unsafe.

**Required negative and ambiguous cases.** At least one actual local service test must demonstrate each type of failure relevant to its assessment: wrong typed/versioned request, digest mismatch, service live-but-unready, overload/drop, timeout after partial work, missing input, training/serving feature drift, stale/denied RAG source cache, authorized versus denied tool action, canary regression, failed rollback postcondition or metric/trace privacy violation. A lesson's optional guided illustration is not by itself another compulsory submission. Every empirical claim must report actual local observations, environment limits and uncertainty distinct from these synthetic arithmetic references.
