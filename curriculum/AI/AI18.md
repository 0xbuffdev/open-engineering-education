## Overview

**AI18 develops robotics intelligence that must act in a physical model, rather than merely produce text or predictions about movement.** A graduate can specify a robot, environment, world and body frames, derive motion and force limits, create a reproducible local physical simulation, interpret noisy sensor data without cheating from hidden ground truth, estimate pose and spatial occupancy, plan collision-aware feasible motion, close a feedback loop under actuator/delay constraints, reason about contact and object manipulation, test embodied imitation/policy learning under shift, and prove independent safe-state behavior. One successful static route or simulated 'move succeeded' message cannot substitute for actual time-stepped dynamics and independently verified state transitions.

**Entry readiness and true boundary.** The **two direct prerequisites are completed [AI6](AI6.md) and [AI8](AI8.md)**. AI6 assesses state/action processes, planning under uncertainty, reward design and reinforcement learning; AI8 assesses visual/depth evidence, geometric coordinates and multimodal perception and transitively inherits neural learning and scientific/statistical foundations. Their prerequisites also supply AI3 symbolic search, AI5 representations, AI2 mathematics, AI1 data and SEF programming. Neither alone covers physical robot frames, kinematics, dynamics, feedback control or contact mechanics: **AI18 teaches those mechanisms explicitly and evaluates them, rather than hiding undergraduate mechanics/control behind an unmentioned SYS/APP/SEC course.** AI9 generative/transformers, AI10 optimization, AI12 tools and AI15 production are **not** separately required; high-level vision-language-action policies are evaluated as an optional/bounded source of proposed goals, not as unrestricted authority. AI18 does **not** replace AI6 general RL, AI8 vision perception, AI3 search, AI13 general evaluation or SYS physical hardware/real-time engineering. The elective is **not required for AI16 graduation** or another AI specialty, and no full robotics, embedded engineering or math department program is silently mandated.

**What the mandatory simulated work actually requires.** Build and run a small, independently reviewable CPU-local 2D physical robot/sensor/control environment or equivalent minimal reproducible simulator—not a collection of path-planning equations masquerading as a working robot. At minimum, the learner must demonstrate an actual timed observe–estimate–plan–act–verify loop, separately logged evaluator world truth, noisy/delayed measurements, bounded actuation, collision-aware trajectory planning, a feedback controller, reliable local stops, and adverse sensor/environment cases. Supporting targeted experiments may use a simple two-link arm, a 1D mass, a cell occupancy map, or a finite object grasp. They are **proportional specialist diagnostics**, not mandatory unrelated production robot applications. A small numeric simulator written in Python, Rust, TypeScript or other inspectable language is sufficient. ROS 2, Gazebo, Drake or hardware-in-the-loop are optional alternatives; no GPU, real robot arm, motors, lidar, camera, drone, lab access, paid physics engine, cloud computer, proprietary dataset or authorized real-world physical operation is required. A simulator may approximate a physical mechanism only when that approximation and missing friction/contact/sensor effects are made explicit; simulator success is not physical-system validation.

**Data, risk, human access and limitations.** Only synthetic/unrestricted scene geometry, bounded simulations and authorized sample data are needed. Learners must never send commands to an actual unsafe machine, manipulate a person without consent or claim real human collision avoidance from toy geometry. Optional image models must be source-permitted; learned behavior in simulation cannot authenticate vision output or human authority. Even under ideal modeled stops, real-world fault tolerance demands independently engineered braking, redundant sensors, electrical power isolation, emergency-stop hardware, mechanical safeguards and qualified hazard review. Public robot safety standards (e.g. current ISO 10218 parts on industrial robot equipment and integration) are **scope-dependent and not a certificate**; learners document applicable-version questions rather than using a paid standard's full text as a compulsory source. Every trajectory/map/perception chart needs a readable numerical/text equivalent, and environmental limits, coordinate conventions, near-miss thresholds and refusal paths must be explicit.

**Research basis and competence progression.** Open Northwestern [Modern Robotics](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/) develops verifiable frame/kinematic, dynamics, planning and control foundations; MIT's [Underactuated Robotics](https://underactuated.csail.mit.edu/) and [Robotic Manipulation](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/) independently expose modeling, feedback and contact limitations. [ROS 2](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.html) and [Gazebo](https://gazebosim.org/libs/sim/) remain optional platforms whose exact clock, frame, queue and physics semantics require real execution evidence, not vendor-default assumptions. The progression follows **body/frame contracts → kinematics and dynamics → bounded sensing/estimation → spatial mapping → geometrically/kinodynamically valid plans → closed-loop control → manipulation/contact → learned-policy shift and robot message authority → observed embodied defense**. A mechanism and its direct mathematical or negative-state check share instruction when they establish one capability; other physically independent obligations remain separate even when that leaves a longer module. No prescribed number of modules, lessons or assessments was applied. The provisional **22–30 weeks** supports actual math derivations, simulator construction, diagnosis, comparison and independent defense, and must not be presented as measured learner workload.

#### Lessons

1. **Embodied intelligence, physical consequences and historical foundations** — Define robot body, environment, sensors, actuator authority, physical time and real state transitions rather than a model's proposed textual motion. Connect rigid-body mechanics, cybernetic feedback, geometric robot planning, mobile localization/SLAM and learned policies to bounded navigation, assisted manipulation, inspection and safe simulated use; explain which new failure each discipline addresses. A plausible plan, textual 'move complete' or successful toy trajectory cannot establish executed motion or safe real-machine autonomy.
2. **One local robot scenario and non-robot baseline** — Choose a simulated differential-drive navigator or arm/object environment, actual goal test and why a fixed script or no robot might be preferable.
3. **Configuration, state, control and observations** — Define q, v, actuator command, sensor observation and hidden ground truth with valid units, bounds, update rates and responsibility for each.
4. **Coordinate-frame naming and physical dimensions** — Distinguish world/map, robot base, sensor, end-effector and object frames and frame origin/direction conventions; reject mixing meters, centimeters and radians.
5. **SE(2) pose composition and inversion** — Work out F1 90-degree rotation and translation, reverse frame transform and frame-path orientation with exact independent point results.
6. **Homogeneous transforms and SE(3) orientation** — Explain 4×4 rigid transforms, orthonormal rotation determinant and quaternion normalization/sign ambiguities without treating Euler parameters as globally nonsingular.
7. **Simulation credibility and hazard boundaries** — Specify collision realism, timestep, missing effects, unknown contacts, physically unreachable states and exclusion of real motors or hazardous deployments.

### Module 2 — Kinematics, Motion Constraints and Robot Actuation

#### Outcomes

- AI18-O2
- AI18-O3
- AI18-O4
- AI18-O13

#### Lessons

1. **Joint spaces, base motion and workspace** — Compare mobile configuration SE(2) with planar revolute arm joint angles and task-space target position, bounds and obstacle geometry.
2. **Planar two-link forward kinematics** — Derive K1 end-effector position from l1,l2, θ1,θ2 and check a concrete right-angle fixture against independent geometry.
3. **Inverse kinematics and multiple solutions** — Calculate law-of-cosines reachability for a target, elbow-up/down configurations and numerical errors near a fully stretched arm.
4. **Arm Jacobian, singular directions and safe velocity limits** — Derive the two-link planar Jacobian from forward-kinematic partial derivatives, independently verify K1's matrix `[[-1,-1],[1,0]]` and the tool velocity `[-1,1] m/s` for joint rates `[1,0] rad/s`. At an extended or folded arm, show Jacobian rank loss and a requested Cartesian direction that cannot be produced locally; identify ill-conditioned inverse velocity, unreachable command and joint-rate/actuator limits. Reject or bound invalid velocity inversion instead of treating a locally linear mapping as unrestricted inverse kinematics.
5. **Wheel-ground rolling assumptions** — Derive differential-drive wheel-to-body forward and inverse kinematics and identify nonholonomic lateral-slip exclusions.
6. **Differential-drive exact constant-twist motion** — Derive M1 straight-line motion at equal wheel speeds and compare turning radius, wheel separation and angular rate when unequal.
7. **Sampled integration and heading wrap** — Compare exact constant-twist and Euler discrete updates for turning, using simulation timestep and angular wrapping without silent clock changes.
8. **Actuator, acceleration and torque boundaries** — Specify wheel rate/steering/joint position/velocity/torque limits and how saturation changes the requested motion.
9. **Kinematic calibration and controlled negative tests** — Perturb wheel radius, base width, encoder resolution and joint zero-offset; show how incorrect calibration appears in observed pose.

### Module 3 — Dynamics, Contact-Free Models and Numerical Simulation

#### Outcomes

- AI18-O5
- AI18-O6
- AI18-O24
- AI18-O29

#### Lessons

1. **Kinematic commands versus actual force-driven acceleration** — Contrast an ideal instantaneous velocity setpoint with a physical body of mass/inertia subject to net force, drag and bounded actuator effort; derive D1's `a=4 N/2 kg=2 m/s²` for a one-dimensional cart from rest. Predict the first `0.1 s` velocity step and explain why a command cannot instantly create unlimited speed or assumed traction. Keep simulation integration error, energy behavior, collision, contact solver and ground-truth clocks as separate tests of this declared physical model.
2. **Time-stepping and energy drift** — Implement explicit Euler and a stable alternative on a simple oscillator or damped cart, vary dt and compare real error/energy behavior.

4. **Discrete collision and tunneling** — Illustrate a small fast object crossing a thin wall between collision samples and explain continuous/swept checks and the limits of tiny timesteps.
5. **Contact assumptions and solver failure** — Differentiate rigid unilateral collision, elastic restitution, friction and model-specific penetration correction; keep unmodeled contact explicit.
6. **Simulator ground truth and observation clocks** — Expose ground truth to evaluator only; provide realistically delayed/noisy sensor samples to agent and log simulation wall-clock versus simulation time.
7. **Deterministic replay and versioned physics** — Pin solver, collision geometry, initial state, integration dt, seed and control sequence; demonstrate a failed replay with changed version.
8. **Unmodeled physical forces and calibration** — Vary mass, rolling resistance, wheel slip or sensor bias across held-out episodes and report deviations without declaring sim-to-real success.

## Completion requirements

### Public exact reference cases and physical/uncertainty limits

Every finite case states its dynamics, geometry, timestep, action constraints, distributions and evaluator truth explicitly. Exact numerical oracles can be checked independently of libraries; they are **not measurements of a real robot**. Required executable evidence separately shows what the learner actually simulated, with sensor/agent access restricted to observations and evaluator scoring based on authoritative world state.

**F1 — A rigid frame transform is directional.** For a planar robot-base origin at world position **(2,1)** and base heading **+π/2 radians** counterclockwise, the point `p_base=(1,0)` maps through `p_world=R(+π/2)p_base+(2,1)` to **(2,2)**. Applying the **inverse** transform `R(−π/2)(p_world−(2,1))` returns **(1,0)**. A direction vector must be rotated but **not translated**; frame and timestamp changes are not interchangeable, and one matrix named without source and target frame is ambiguous.

**K1 — Planar arm forward kinematics and Jacobian.** A two-revolute planar arm with **l1=l2=1 m**, `θ1=0`, `θ2=π/2`, standard joint order, has end-effector `x=l1 cosθ1+l2 cos(θ1+θ2)=1`, `y=l1 sinθ1+l2 sin(θ1+θ2)=1`. Differentiating gives **J=[[-1,−1],[1,0]]** (meters/radian at this configuration); for joint angular velocity `[1,0]` radians/s, predicted Cartesian tool velocity is **[−1,1] m/s**. At a fully extended arm `θ1=0,θ2=0`, the Jacobian loses rank (determinant0), so an arbitrary Cartesian velocity may be infeasible and the inverse solution can be ill-conditioned. IK must also reject points beyond total reach **2 m** and invalid joint limits.

**M1 — Differential-drive no-slip kinematics.** With wheel radius **r=.1m**, wheel-center separation **b=.5m**, right/left angular velocities each **2 rad/s** under equal wheel conditions, body linear velocity is `v=r(ωR+ωL)/2=.2m/s` and body yaw rate `ω=r(ωR−ωL)/b=0`; from a fixed straight orientation, two seconds of this ideal constant motion gives **.4m** traveled. Nonzero differential rotation requires heading-integrated curved motion, not simple straight-line displacement, and the no-lateral-slip constraint is an **assumption**, not a test of actual traction.

**D1 — Force-driven finite step.** An ideal 1D cart with mass **2 kg** and constant net force **4 N** has acceleration **a=F/m=2 m/s²**. From rest, explicit Euler integration of velocity with **Δt=.1s** produces **v1=.2 m/s**. At the initial position x0=0, explicit Euler position using **old** v0=0 gives x1=0, while the exact constant-acceleration position after .1s is .01m. This is a deliberately observable integration error; it does not imply a universal integrator/solver stability guarantee.

**B1 — Bayesian sensor belief and evidence independence.** One cell is occupied with prior **P(O)=.2**; a sensor hit has likelihood **P(hit|O)=.8**, **P(hit|not O)=.1**. After **one** hit, Bayes yields posterior **P(O|hit)=(.2×.8)/(.2×.8+.8×.1)=2/3**. Reusing the same physical sensor observation twice as if independent is unjustified. A map and robot pose posterior are different claims and measurement model/source/time validity remains essential.

**E1 — One scalar Kalman update.** A 1D prior state estimate has mean **μ=0**, covariance **P=4** in squared units; a compatible observation **z=2** uses identity measurement H=1 and independent measurement noise covariance **R=1**. The scalar gain is **K=P/(P+R)=4/5**. Posterior mean is **μ+=0+(4/5)(2−0)=1.6**, posterior covariance under this ideal model is **P+=(1−4/5)4=.8**. This is a single correction, not an entire mobile-robot filter. Correlated noise, a different prior or stale observation invalidates the numbers.

**G1 — Occupancy in log odds.** Start with **P(O)=.5**, hence log prior odds0. A sensor observation with occupied-versus-free likelihood ratio **3** contributes log likelihood ratio **ln3**, yielding posterior odds3 and **P(O|observation)=3/(1+3)=.75**. Multiple *independent* observations can add log likelihood increments; repeated data from the same measurement or incorrect pose association must not be double-counted as independent evidence. Unknown cells have no justified geometric clearance.

**P1 — Graph path costs and physical feasibility.** A directed graph has edges `S→A=1`, `A→G=5`, `S→B=2`, `B→G=2`, `A→B=1`, all nonnegative. A shortest path cost is **4**, attained by both `S→B→G` and `S→A→B→G`; an implementation returning either may be correct. A* with `h=0` is Dijkstra, and a heuristic must not overestimate true remaining cost for admissibility. Separately, a robot with **0.2m** circular radius cannot pass through an unyielding **0.3m-wide** straight corridor because its **0.4m** diameter exceeds the free passage: a collision-free *point path* is not a collision-free robot path. Dynamic, kinodynamic and uncertain-world constraints need their own checks.

**C1 — Sampled feedback and action bounds.** For scalar integrator `x_{k+1}=x_k+Δt u_k`, reference **r=1**, initial **x0=0**, sampled gain **K=2 s⁻¹** and **Δt=.1 s**, proportional request `u0=K(r−x0)=2` (state-units/s) gives unbounded **x1=.2**. With actual actuator bound **|u|≤1**, the command is **u0=1** and **x1=.1** instead. Unbounded closed-loop error multiplier is **1−KΔt=.8**, assuming ideal fresh feedback; it is *not* a general stability guarantee under saturation/delay. For a **separate** one-step unconstrained cost `J(u)=(x+u−1)²+u²`, derivative zero gives **u*=(1−x)/2**; this toy optimizer cannot bypass real actuator/collision constraints.

**H1 — Contact friction has narrow assumptions.** A toy object has mass **1 kg**, gravity **g=10 m/s²**, so downward weight **10 N**. Two parallel fingertips exert independent equal normal forces **N=10 N each**, each with **static friction coefficient μ=.5**, giving total maximum upward static friction **2μN=10 N**—*exactly* the object weight under vertical symmetric no-slip idealization. Reducing normal force to **8 N each** gives maximum **8 N**, insufficient to support the object. Equal capacity and weight is only a marginal theoretical limit without dynamics, moment/geometry uncertainty or safety margin. Actual object-attached/lift/release state must be verified from simulated contact, not from a model's narration.

**R1 — Robot outcome versus plan, and control stop.** In a bounded five-episode simulation, suppose the independently instrumented world reports **3 reached-goal episodes**, **1 collision episode**, and **1 stalled/timeout episode** (mutually exclusive terminal statuses by this rubric). Then observed completion fraction is **3/5**, collision fraction **1/5**, and incomplete-without-collision fraction **1/5**, with distinct denominators and labeled initial conditions. A planning success bit, reward, controller's text or code test passing is not the world outcome; this synthetic table does not estimate population-wide robot safety. A watchdog/clearance gate that denies a command must cause **zero physical simulated actuation after its declared deadline**, not merely return an error string.

**Safety and reproducibility qualification.** A physical E-stop, industrial risk assessment, certified braking/force limit, sensory redundancy or safe real-human interaction is **not** demonstrated by a CPU process returning zero velocity. All mandatory tests operate only on bounded local synthetic environments. Real human-robot work requires qualified supervision, hardware-specific controls, maintenance/calibration and applicable laws/standards; AI18 cannot certify ISO compliance or autonomous deployment safety. Claims about sim-to-real transfer, physical grasping or actual environment quality remain unverified without real, permitted independent physical testing.
