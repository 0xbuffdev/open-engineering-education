## Overview

**AI10 studies how to adapt an existing neural/generative model correctly and efficiently under a legitimate training signal, finite resources and a credible evaluation boundary.** A learner must decide *whether* an update is warranted, build source-safe loss-bearing examples, derive optimizer and accumulation behavior, diagnose mixed precision and memory tradeoffs, choose a full/selective/low-rank update, verify instruction and bounded offline-preference objectives, evaluate quantized training constraints and compute scaling/replica semantics, then demonstrate a real justified model-training decision on a small CPU experiment. A framework configuration, lower training loss or shorter gradient trace does not itself establish improved generalization or useful alignment.

**Scientific context and application.** Stochastic optimization, weight decay, learning-rate scheduling, activation recomputation, adaptive low-rank updates and distributed data/model parallelism arise because modern neural models have different costs for parameters, gradients, optimizer states, activations and communication. Domain adaptation, instruction tuning and finite reference-relative preference objectives change what data and conditional targets are fitted; compression and quantization change the represented numbers and sometimes the executable kernels. These techniques are useful for modest local adaptation as well as larger systems, but there is no general guarantee that fewer trainable parameters means faster iteration, that lower precision preserves quality, or that preference optimization means safety or factuality.

**Prerequisite and competency ownership.** Entry requires completed [AI9](AI9.md). Through it, learners have assessed [AI8](AI8.md) vision/multimodal data, [AI7](AI7.md) text tokenization and retrieval evaluation, [AI5](AI5.md) differentiable computation, gradient-safe training, frozen-feature transfer and optimizers, [AI4](AI4.md) generalization/evaluation, [AI2](AI2.md) calculus/likelihood, [AI1](AI1.md) scientific data and completed SEF. AI9 already assesses transformer/attention mechanics, teacher-forced versus masked generation objectives and small transformer training. **AI10 does not repeat** Python basics, generic backpropagation, basic layer construction, classical cross-validation, text segmentation, transformer architecture or generative model taxonomy. It newly assesses *adaptation-specific data and loss masking, correct optimizer states and schedule/batch mathematics, precision/activation memory, full and low-rank adaptation, assistant-only and finite offline-preference objectives, quantization, distributed effective batches and robust replay of an actual training intervention*. [AI6](AI6.md) reinforcement learning is not required: full reward-model RLHF, PPO and a real online RL policy are not AI10 completion requirements. No APP/SYS course is required to solve the published CPU-scale learning and resource oracles.

**Neighboring course boundaries.** AI11 owns retrieval-augmented generation pipelines and grounded output; AI12 tool-using agent systems; AI13 full AI-system assurance/evaluation; AI14 systematic security/ethics/governance; AI15 deployed serving, monitoring and MLOps. AI10 covers immediate training-side evidence, data permissions, model-update verification and limited preference math, not production infrastructure, global safety certification, cloud orchestration or instruction-tuned frontier models.

**Environment and evidence constraints.** Mandatory evidence uses small synthetic/open licensed examples and a tiny source-trained or permitted-weight model runnable on an ordinary CPU. Python and PyTorch or other open tensor tools are convenient but not mandatory. Low-precision accelerators, multi-GPU FSDP and low-bit compute kernels may be examined using explicit arithmetic/simulated rank partitions and actual supported local experiments; no GPU, foundation-model download, remote accelerator, private data or paid service is a requirement. Disclose actual device, package versions, dtype, tokenizer/model IDs, optimizer/schedule/scaler state, gradient reduction, effective batch, split/source grouping, model/adapter licenses, resource caps, random-state handling and failed experiments. Numerical matrices and plots need equivalent text/table alternatives. Authentic measured performance must be separated from toy exact oracles and theoretical memory estimates.

**Design derivation.** The instructional modules arise from distinct dependencies: valid data/targets before optimizer updates; update semantics before numerical/memory controls; precision and memory knowledge before an adaptation-policy choice; model adaptation before instruction/preference comparisons; quantization before distributed budgets; and all of them before a defensible decision. There is **no fixed count target** for modules, lessons or assessments; individual lessons are combined or separated only for coherent mechanisms and assessable differences. Six cumulative summative evaluations measure nineteen capabilities with shared evidence and one assessor-supplied unfamiliar variation. The 16–22-week estimate is provisional and will be revised from actual teaching and assessment workload; no source CI implies learner-tested readiness.

- **AI10-O12:** Derive and validate quantization/dequantization, code range, clipping, zero point, rounding, group calibration and PTQ versus QAT distinctions.

## Modules

### Module 1 — Adaptation Questions, Data Rights and Training Targets

#### Outcomes

- AI10-O1
- AI10-O2
- AI10-O3
- AI10-O18

#### Lessons

1. **Determine whether learning is warranted** — Compare an unchanged model, a data/rule correction and a trained adaptation for one bounded objective; define the intended behavior and opportunity cost.
2. **Identify inherited model and data contracts** — Record base weights, tokenizer revision, permitted dataset, subject/source IDs and the output distribution that the task legitimately measures.
3. **Data lineage before chunking** — Split by document, subject or session before constructing near-duplicate chunks; show how post-chunk random splitting leaks source content.
4. **Supervision tokens and response regions** — Trace beginning/end markers, prompt content, completion and padding in a finite token sequence; define the exact target positions.
5. **Sequence packing and example isolation** — Form two short packed examples with explicit causal block boundaries, disallowing response tokens from an unrelated record as context.
6. **Deduplication, contamination and rights** — Distinguish legitimate near duplicates from source leakage; reject a withdrawn, unlicensed or evaluation-only record and explain the changed population.
7. **Objective and held-out baseline** — Specify completion-only versus full-token likelihood, no-update control, final test freeze, a negative case and what model improvement would actually demonstrate.

### Module 2 — Optimization Updates, Schedules and Microbatch Mathematics

#### Outcomes

- AI10-O4
- AI10-O5
- AI10-O16

#### Lessons

1. **Parameter and optimizer state as different objects** — Trace w, gradient, moments and scheduler step in one update; contrast a frozen base parameter with an optimizer-owned adapter parameter.
2. **Decoupled decay and adaptive moments** — Derive O1 AdamW scalar moment estimates and first corrected update, naming the exact epsilon, reduction and decay point.
3. **Bias correction beyond the first step** — Inspect a second-step stored-moment trace and show why reinitializing optimizer state mid-training changes the trajectory.
4. **Update-indexed schedules** — Define finite warmup and decay by optimizer update number rather than examples, epochs or microbatch calls; calculate one changed schedule boundary.
5. **Parameter-group policies** — Apply stated rates and decay exclusions to adapter/head versus frozen, bias and normalization subsets; reject undocumented universal defaults.
6. **Accumulate unequal microbatch gradients** — Derive A1 global weighted mean rather than naive average of two means, including loss-scaling implications for different sample counts.
7. **Update lifecycle and accidental extra steps** — Separate zeroing, forward, backward, unscale, clip, step and scheduler advance; diagnose a microbatch-level premature update.
8. **Norm clipping under explicit reduction** — Compute N1's gradient vector norm and threshold projection without mistaking scaled gradients for the true unscaled update.
9. **NaN, too-large rate and stale gradients** — Locate a controlled nonfinite or unstable update in real traces and choose skip, lower step or safe failure based on observed evidence.
10. **Fair optimization experiments** — Compare two actual schedules or effective batches using identical training-token budgets, held-out selection and recorded compute, without guaranteeing a winning recipe.

## Completion requirements

### Shared public mathematical and failure reference cases

These oracles specify **finite assumptions**, not universal performance targets. Reviewers may substitute equally public independently derivable cases. A library printout without an independent derivation does not establish the relevant outcome.

**S1 — Completion-only supervised targets.** On tokens `[BOS,P,A,EOS]` the correct next-token inputs are `[BOS,P,A]` and labels `[P,A,EOS]`. For an objective excluding prompt token P but including A and EOS, use loss mask `[0,1,1]`. If probabilities for the two correct supervised targets are respectively **1/2 and 1/4**, sum NLL=**ln8**, mean supervised-token NLL=**ln8/2**, and perplexity=**sqrt8**. The exact scalar probability exercise inherits AI9 fundamentals; the *new tested skill* is correct assistant/completion-only masking, packed-record source boundaries, valid denominators and train-only fitting. In a packed pair of **independent examples**, each example must be causally isolated (e.g., block-diagonal visibility); full-sequence training with intentional continuation across records would be a **different** declared objective. Empty valid-target sets must not be divided by zero.

**O1 — Explicit AdamW first step.** A scalar w0=2 has gradient g=1/2, learning rate η=1/10, decoupled weight decay λ=1/5, β1=9/10, β2=999/1000, epsilon=10^-8, and initial moments m0=v0=0. Then m1=**1/20**, v1=**1/4000**, bias-corrected mhat1=**1/2**, vhat1=**1/4**. With defined decoupled update w1=(1−ηλ)w0−η mhat1/(sqrt(vhat1)+epsilon), **w1=49/25−(1/10)(1/2)/(1/2+10^-8) ≈ 1.860000002**. Its weight-decay contribution is **−1/25**. An optimizer that inserts λw into the gradient before Adam normalization or resets the moments at a continuation step is **not** performing this same update.

**A1 — Unequal batch accumulation.** First microbatch holds **one** example with gradient2, second holds **three** examples each with gradient4. True per-example mean gradient is **(1×2+3×4)/4=7/2**, not naive unweighted microbatch-mean average **3**. Under one plain-SGD step from w0=1 with η=1/10, correct w1 is **13/20=0.65** versus incorrect **0.7**. For two *equal-size* microbatches with means 2 and4, the mean is3; using unscaled sum6 would double the intended step. Loss reduction and actual optimizer-step timing must be declared.

**N1 — Gradient scale, unscale and clipping.** Unscaled g=(3,4) has Euclidean norm **5**; max-norm2 clips it to **(6/5,8/5)**. A hypothetical loss scale of **8** stores (24,32), which must be unscaled **before** the stated norm-2 clip. Nonfinite-value detection may cause a scaling implementation to skip the optimizer step; if a step is skipped, logs must not silently count it as a successful parameter update. Device-specific half-precision behavior must be observed rather than fabricated from this exact vector.

**L1 — Low-rank adapter shapes, freeze and merge.** Frozen base W has shape **4×6**. Rank r=1, alpha=1: B is the 4×1 column (1,0,2,−1); A is the 1×6 row (1,0,−1,0,2,1). Then ΔW=(alpha/r)BA has rows **(1,0,−1,0,2,1)**, **(0,0,0,0,0,0)**, **(2,0,−2,0,4,2)**, **(−1,0,1,0,−2,−1)**. Adapter parameters number **4+6=10** versus **24** in the dense frozen W; extra biases/heads/optimizer states are excluded. For column input x=(1,0,0,0,0,0), ΔWx=**(1,0,2,−1)**. Zero B and nonzero A initialize ΔW=0 while allowing an initial B gradient; zeroing *both* factors may prevent a first-order update. Merging (W+ΔW) reproduces the deterministic float adapted result within declared tolerance **only when base/adapter revisions, scaling and relevant dtype/state agree**.

**P1 — Reference-relative finite pairwise preference.** For the *same prompt*, chosen/rejected complete-response log probabilities are logπθ(c)=−0.2, logπθ(r)=−1.2 and fixed reference logπref(c)=−0.4, logπref(r)=−0.6. With beta=1 and DPO-style loss `−log sigmoid(beta[(logπθ(c)−logπθ(r))−(logπref(c)−logπref(r))])`, target log-odds gap=**1**, reference gap=**1/5**, margin=**4/5**, loss=**ln(1+exp(−4/5)) ≈ 0.3711006659**. These are legitimate stipulated sequence log probabilities under a consistent vocabulary/normalization, **not** a normalized binary distribution over just c/r. Real preference labels need their own rights, context and disagreement contract; this math does not prove safe, honest or preferred output on a new population and does not require RLHF policy training.

**Q1 — Signed symmetric quantization and saturation.** With code range **[−127,127]**, scale s=**1/4**, zero point0, q=clip(round(w/s),−127,127), reconstructed value sq and a named nearest-integer policy, **0.63→q3→0.75** (error+0.12), **−0.37→q−1→−0.25** (error+0.12), **40→q127→31.75** (error−8.25). A 24-element int8 matrix needs **24 bytes of integer values plus scale/metadata**, compared to **96 bytes for float32 values alone**. This excludes trainable LoRA weights, optimizer states, activations and dequantization buffers. Rounding is not exactly differentiable; QAT may deliberately use an approximate straight-through gradient, not a mathematical gradient of round.

**D1 — Rank aggregation and memory.** Equal independent worker example-mean gradients2 and4 give global example mean **3**, not sum6. If the workers hold one and three observations respectively, their correctly weighted global example mean is **7/2**, not equal-rank mean3, absent an explicitly different objective, padding or dropping. A single float32 trainable parameter, gradient and two equally sized Adam moment entries consume **4+4+8=16 bytes** in a minimal arithmetic ledger, before activations, master copies and allocation overhead. Ordinary replicated DDP ranks can each hold this full state; ideal half sharding of selected components reduces those components alone and requires data movement/reconstruction, not automatic halving of total memory or doubling of throughput.

**R1 — Resumable versus inference-only state.** A genuine continuation record may need model/base and adapter revision, optimizer moment buffers, scheduler update index, scale controller, data order/cursor, RNG and any partially accumulated gradients. An inference-only saved weight file can reproduce a fixed prediction but cannot establish the same next optimizer update. When a reviewer changes target mask, batch weighting, LoRA factor, quantization range, worker partition or revokes a training source, independently predict or identify the missing information for the new result and verify repair/refusal. Exact bitwise replay is not promised across unsupported library/device arithmetic.

**Common invalid/boundary cases:** no loss-bearing tokens; prompt-to-response leak; packed cross-record causal leak; duplicate source across split; unauthorized source/weights; incorrect Adam schedule index; nonfinite gradient; unscaled clipping; frozen model with no trained path; adapter rank/target or base-tokenizer mismatch; zero/negative quantization scale; unsupported low-bit kernel/merge; unweighted unequal-rank mean; and a checkpoint missing optimizer/RNG state. All exact claims depend on declared shapes, sample counts, reductions, ordering and versions; practical models require **actual** measured results and limits.
