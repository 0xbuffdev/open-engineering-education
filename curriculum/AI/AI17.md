## Overview

**AI17 teaches how sampled audio carries evidence about sound events, human speech and temporal sequences—and how to build, evaluate and responsibly constrain systems that interpret or synthesize that evidence.** Graduates must reason from a waveform and microphone/source clock through faithful acoustic representations, annotation and grouping, VAD/events, genuine learned acoustic features, CTC-based speech recognition and transcripts, anonymous speaker turns, bounded denoising and speech synthesis, streaming inference, and audio-specific rights, fairness, spoof/identity limits and unfamiliar-case repair. A plausible transcription, a frequency plot, a similar voice embedding or a natural-sounding synthesized clip is **not** independent proof of correct words, true identity, consent, authenticity or safe deployment.

**History, applications and scope.** Physical acoustic pressure is sampled and encoded; digital signal processing supplies frequency- and time-domain observations; probabilistic speech systems align variable acoustic sequences with written symbols; modern self-supervised representations and conditional sequence models extend recognition, speech-language interaction and synthesis. Applications include captioning and accessibility, bounded meeting transcription with consent, noise-robust audio-event detection, permitted text-to-speech output, speaker-*turn* segmentation, limited environmental-sound monitoring and audio content inspection. **Speech recognition**, **acoustic event tagging**, **speaker diarization**, **biometric identification**, **speech translation**, **enhancement** and **speech synthesis** have different target definitions, ethical constraints, evaluation populations and success claims. The course studies these related tasks deeply enough to recognize valid and invalid evidence but does **not** pretend a short CPU model has solved commercial multilingual ASR or natural voice generation.

**Preparation and novelty.** The **only direct prerequisite is completed [AI9](AI9.md)**, which supplies transformer/generative sequence modeling and inherits [AI7](AI7.md) natural-language processing and [AI5](AI5.md) neural representation/learning, plus appropriate earlier mathematical/scientific foundations. Although AI9's current course graph transitively includes visual AI8, no vision, reinforcement learning, APP/SYS/SEC specialization, AI10 finetuning, AI13 general system TEVV or AI15 production-platform course is a *separate* requirement for this elective. AI7 already assesses written-language labels and text error reasoning; AI8 image–text representation and multimodal pairing; AI9 attention and sequence generation; AI13 general evaluation reliability; AI14 governance. **AI17 alone owns sampling/acoustics, speech activity, sound-event taxonomies, audio-specific ASR alignment and word metrics, real speaker-turn timing, cautious spoken-output synthesis, controlled acoustic degradation and streaming-audio failure semantics.** Existing mathematics/neural/IR knowledge is reused, not re-taught as unrelated filler. This advanced audio elective can be taken when its actual predecessor is complete; **AI17 is not a prerequisite for AI16, core AI graduation or the other AI electives**.

**Required environment and real audio evidence.** All compulsory operations can run on an ordinary CPU using a tiny Python/TypeScript/Rust or equivalent inspectable signal-processing and machine-learning toolchain, with openly generated numerical tones/silence, a deliberately small open/licensed natural-speech sample for actual ASR, and carefully permissioned *non-identifying* simulated speaker-turn data or accessible licensed multi-voice clips. No microphone, recording of the learner's or a stranger's voice, consent-collection field study, voice cloning, proprietary evaluation set, paid ASR/TTS API, training of a large Whisper/wav2vec model, GPU, hosted account, biometric enrollment or internet-scale dataset is mandatory. A reproducible small speech subset (e.g. legitimately downloaded and locally pinned utterances with appropriate Common Voice release/license conditions) must be identified with true source IDs; generated toy phonemes are **not** falsely presented as real-world speech accuracy. Alternatives may supply legally equivalent clips without mandating a particular repository, language or dataset license. Local synthetic *sound-event, turn, denoising and synthesis* cases may accompany the primary actual-speech task. All tests involving identity or attacks are harmless simulations with **no real protected speakers or unauthorized impersonation**. Accessible transcript and quantitative text alternatives accompany every required audio artifact; where subjective listening is not genuinely performed, the report must explicitly withhold intelligibility/naturalness claims.

**Research and source uncertainty.** Original [CTC research](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2006/graves2006icml-connectionist/) explains summing across latent time alignments; [wav2vec 2.0](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/92d1e1eb1cd6f9fba3227870bb6d7f07-Abstract.html) and [SpecAugment](https://research.google/pubs/specaugment-a-simple-augmentation-method-for-automatic-speech-recognition/) illustrate modern representation/preprocessing approaches without requiring their industrial-scale implementation. The [NIST SCTK/sclite scorer](https://github.com/usnistgov/SCTK) makes transcript alignment/normalization conventions explicit; [DIHARD scoring definitions](https://dihardchallenge.github.io/dihard1/overview.html) specify diarization, collar and overlap policies. [Common Voice datasets](https://commonvoice.mozilla.org/en) and [ESC-50 source](https://github.com/karolpiczak/ESC-50) are optional data discovery references, **not unconditional permission**: versions, recordings, source licenses, NC restrictions and reuse conditions differ. An audio taxonomy such as [AudioSet ontology](https://research.google.com/audioset/ontology/) does not itself license the sounds associated with labels. Generated speech and speaker embeddings raise separate identity/copyright and privacy questions; a detector's output is not an authenticity certificate.

**Competency-first architecture and pacing.** We first identify the physically correct audio evidence/annotation chain; the mechanism-dependent tasks of voice activity and acoustic event representation; alignment and recognition; speaker-time boundaries; denoising and generated speech; and finally realistic stream robustness, language inclusion, source rights and independent transfer. These exit capabilities determine the instructional stages and proportional experiments. **No target number of modules, lessons, tests, labs or assessment IDs** was used, and no lesson→test→lab template is assumed. The mandatory source/evidence may be shared across assessments with a single coherent study, not separated into seven unrelated production projects. The provisional **18–26 weeks** reflects actual sample integrity, DSP derivations, model experiments, error analysis, controlled listening/synthesis limits and technical defense; it is **not** evidence of learner-calibrated teaching readiness.

## Completion requirements

### Public independent waveform, speech and streaming reference cases

**A1 — Clock, samples, PCM and spectral aliases.** A legitimate mono PCM waveform at **8,000 samples/s** with duration **0.25 s** contains exactly **2,000 samples**. If each sample is 16-bit signed PCM, raw payload has **4,000 bytes** (without WAV/container metadata); an otherwise identical two-channel payload has **8,000 bytes**. Signed 16-bit PCM spans **−32768 through 32767** before floating-point scaling. If a **3 kHz** sinusoid sampled at **8 kHz** is naively decimated to **4 kHz** without bandlimited filtering, its discrete frequency aliases to magnitude **1 kHz**, not preserved 3 kHz content. A valid conversion must filter out-band energy; relabeling 8 kHz samples as 4 kHz metadata is *not* resampling. Exact quantization and alias-phase behavior depend on signal/filter conventions.

**S1 — Short-time spectral arithmetic without a library.** Under the DFT definition `X[k]=sum_{n=0}^{3} x[n] exp(-j 2π kn/4)`, the real four-sample sequence **x=[1,0,−1,0]** has complex bins **X=[0,2,0,2]** and magnitude **[0,2,0,2]**. For non-rectangular windows or another FFT normalization, numbers differ. A frequency-magnitude plot alone does not prove a phoneme/speaker; phase and window choices matter for inverse reconstruction.

**V1 — Voice/sound activity is an explicit threshold.** Four independently precomputed consecutive frame-RMS values are **[0.10, 0.05, 0.40, 0.20]**. For an inclusive threshold of **0.20**, flagged frames are **3 and 4** (1-based indexing). These numbers are *synthetic frame features*; passing the test does not mean RMS detects human speech reliably in music/noise/overlap or establishes word boundaries.

**E1 — Multi-label event frame agreement.** For one fully judged event class over five equal-duration frames, true membership `[0,1,1,0,1]`, hypothesis `[0,1,0,1,1]`. Then **TP=2, FP=1, FN=1, TN=1**; precision **2/3**, recall **2/3**, and frame intersection-over-union **2/(2+1+1)=1/2**. This is not a clip-level F1 or a score for an entire overlapping multi-class ontology; event-onset tolerance and unknown reference labels would require another declared contract.

**C1 — CTC includes path marginalization.** For exactly two independent framewise distributions over **{blank, a}**, frame1 `P(a)=0.6, P(blank)=0.4`, frame2 `P(a)=0.7, P(blank)=0.3`. The CTC collapse **first merges consecutive repeated non-blank labels, then removes blanks**. The three paths mapping to target `a` are **(a,a)** probability **0.42**, **(a,blank)** probability **0.18**, **(blank,a)** probability **0.28**, so **P(target 'a')=0.88**. Both blanks give **P(empty)=0.12**. For three frames, **(a,blank,a) → 'aa'**, while **(a,a,blank) → 'a'**. CTC assumptions and frame independence are a model's factorization contract, not a claim that real acoustics are independent.

**W1 — Transcript alignment and reference denominators.** For the exact lowercase whitespace-word reference **“we can hear sound”** and hypothesis **“we hear sounds”**, one valid minimal alignment deletes “can” and substitutes “sound”→“sounds”, giving word edit distance **2** and **WER=(S+D+I)/N=(1+1+0)/4=1/2**. A **separate** character-level example reference “cat” versus hypothesis “cut” has **CER=1/3**. Valid alternative minimal alignments can apportion substitution/deletion differently for other sequences; the total minimum distance and declared normalization are primary. An empty reference word count does not license division by zero, and a transcript with ambiguous reference cannot be assigned a fabricated correct answer.

**D1 — Speaker diarization with a declared error denominator.** With a **nonoverlapping five-second** recording, reference speaker A occupies half-open interval **[0,2)s**, reference B **[2,5)s**. Predicted A **[0,1)s**, predicted B **[1,5)s**. Under optimal global speaker permutation, **no collar, no speech misses, no false alarms and no overlap**, **1 second** of speaker confusion over **5 seconds** of reference speech gives **DER=1/5=0.20**. If an overlap region, nonzero collar or missing/false-alarm segment is introduced, the scoring definition and numerical result must be recomputed; an anonymous speaker tag is not an authenticated person's identity.

**N1 — Enhancement SNR and distortion.** For known clean `s=[1,0,−1,0]` and additive input noise `n=[0,1,0,1]`, signal energy **2** and noise energy **2**, so **input SNR=10log10(2/2)=0 dB**. If the output is `s+e` with remaining error `e=[0,0.5,0,0.5]` (energy **0.5**), then **output SNR=10log10(2/0.5)=10log10(4)≈6.0206 dB**. These are explicitly known-clean squared-error energy calculations, not a reference-free estimate or proof of phonetic intelligibility. Different separation scales, source silence, phase and perceptual distortions can make a single SNR score misleading.

**T1 — Chunk duration and real-time factors.** At **16,000 samples/s**, a block of **320 samples** carries **0.02 seconds (20 ms)** of audio. Three consecutive nonoverlapping such blocks represent **60 ms** of source timeline, *not automatically* 60 ms wall-clock transcription completion. For a separate hypothetical run taking **0.03 s processor time** to handle **0.10 s** of audio, defined real-time factor **RTF=0.03/0.10=0.3**. Actual RTF, start-up, queueing, lookahead and final-turn latency require measured timestamps and a pinned CPU and cannot be invented from sample counts.

**P1 — Rights, source and identity fail-closed cases.** A nominally fluent generated word, speaker label or accent classification is not permission to reveal a recording, identify a person or synthesize an individual's recognizable voice. A synthetically altered pitch or audio-container metadata does not itself authenticate, watermark or deidentify a person; a missing right/consent/source revision makes the required action **deny, abstain or escalate**, while permitted raw audio access and retained derived embeddings must have explicit purpose and minimization. Use only synthetic, fully authorized sound for adversarial demonstrations.

**Evidence requirement.** Each exact fixture must be independently derived without taking the test library's output as its only oracle. Every real audio/speech model metric must be measured on actual, permissibly used source waveforms with a frozen transcript/event/turn reference, stated sample source/group, normalization, model and data revisions, CPU environment and denominators. The course may require *no new human listening study*; if naturalness or intelligibility is not independently judged, its status is unknown. Speaker diarization metrics never prove human identity and a spoofing test never proves global security.
